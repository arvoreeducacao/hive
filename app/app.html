        Shortcuts

        Look

        These live in ~/.hive/config.jsonc, together with the keyboard shortcuts. Edit the file by hand and the app follows it while it runs; change it here and the file changes, keeping your comments. Leave a field empty to go back to the default.

        Performance

        Lean is the default: nothing slides or pulses, there is no blur behind the panels, and the screen has less to paint. Full brings the motion and the blur back, and costs memory and battery, more so on a computer with 8 or 16 GB.

        Either way, when nobody is looking at the window the motion pauses on its own.

        Meetings

        The words are written on this machine. Only the text goes to the model that writes the notes.

        A browser extension hands the captions of a Meet call to the hive, with the name of who spoke. While they arrive they are what gets written down; the microphone and the computer's sound keep being heard and take over if they stop.

        Chrome, Edge, Brave
        1. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode, at the top right.
        2. Click Load unpacked.
        3. Pick the chrome folder below. It sits in your home folder, under hive-meet-captions.
        Firefox, Zen
        1. Open about:debugging and choose This Firefox.
        2. Click Load Temporary Add-on.
        3. Pick manifest.json inside the firefox folder below. Firefox forgets it when it closes.
        Then open a Meet call: a Record button shows up beside the call's own buttons.

        Your character

        your face in the hivethe body and the colour are yours alone — the screen is free, and so is what it wears

        The eyes are the little you at the foot of the rail — it has to be on above for them to have anywhere to look. Anywhere on the screen means it keeps following you into the window you moved to, and stops while the hive is minimised or hidden.

        Notices

        Three moments: needs you is a seat that stopped and is waiting on you, answered is a seat that just finished a turn you have not read — those two have a switch and a sound of your choosing. The third is someone on the team knocking on one of your keyboards, and it nudges: the window shakes and the ask lands in the middle of the screen, with the sound from MSN. That one has no switch and keeps that sound: it is a person waiting on the other side, and silence there is a promise nobody kept. The desktop notice itself goes out silent — this is the sound you hear. Press play to try one on, and use the volume when the system notification sound is too shy to hear.

        always on

        Off, and nobody on the team can reach you: the button to ask for your keyboard leaves your seats on their side, a poke or a hi goes nowhere, and a question sent through one of their chats comes straight back to them, unanswered. Your cards stay on the cluster as they were — this is about who can interrupt you, not who can see you.

        The screen

        It works in blocks of a few chats each — the field below says how many seats one block holds, from 2 to 6; 4 is the ceiling of what one person holds at once. A new chat takes a free seat in the block you are on; once it fills up, the next one opens a new block. No chat is ever pulled out of its block on its own — the one exception is a block that fits whole into the one before it, and then the two join instead of leaving you with half a screen. A block you arranged by hand is never touched. Every block carries a label saying what it is about — click it to rewrite it.

        Blocks live inside a workspace — the subject you are on, which holds as many blocks as it needs and keeps its own colour. The pile of floors opens on the name at the top left, and the same key closes it; a chat you open lands in the workspace you are looking at, and a workspace you close hands its blocks to the one before it, never closing a chat.

        Side by side lays the whole block out in a single row, room a wide screen has and a grid wastes; on a narrow window both settle into one column. The status of a chat starts folded away so the terminal takes the room — the minus on the card brings it back.

        Inside the PR panel, ↑↓ changes file, v marks it seen and moves on, shift v goes back and unmarks the last one, and [ ] changes PR. A path to an image printed in a tile is clickable: it opens the picture over the screen — on your machine or on the server, esc closes.

        A picture a chat names in its answer hangs under the words as a thumbnail. Every shot a chat takes lives in ~/.hive/shots/<chat>/; the folder goes when the chat is closed, and anything older than seven days is swept when the hive starts. measuring the shots…

        The pod screen shows the state of the machine the hive lives on — memory, disk, Claude's login, environment variables — with a root terminal at the bottom. Sleeping, restarting and pushing the .env ask for confirmation because they kill the cloud sessions.

        The conversation

        Everything a seat has read since it opened is read again on every answer, so a seat left open for days gets slower and dearer each turn. The field below says how far a conversation runs before it is summed up and carried on from the summary.

        k

        Empty leaves the choice to the agent; 100k to 1000k sets your own. A lower ceiling sums up more often — cheaper and quicker, at the cost of reading again what it still needs. A seat takes a new ceiling when it comes back, not while it runs.

        Every native chat takes the ceiling, on Claude, Codex, Kimi, Kiro or Cursor; a terminal takes it only on Claude. The field is off while none of yours does.

        Closing quiet chats

        days

        Empty keeps every chat open until you close it. A number from 1 to 90 closes, once an hour, every chat on this machine that nobody wrote to and that did nothing for that many days. A chat that is working or waiting for you is never closed. The closed chat goes to the archive with its whole conversation, and comes back from there with one click.

        Clearing closed chats' history

        days

        An empty field keeps 30. Once an hour, the screen history of every closed chat that did nothing for that many days is cleared from this machine, freeing disk. Only what can come back goes: a Claude chat whose conversation is already on GitHub through cloud-sessions, or a Codex chat, which keeps its own history. Reopened from the archive, the chat brings its conversation back on screen. Kimi, Kiro, OpenCode and Cursor chats are never cleared. 0 turns this off.

        Slack

        Connect this hive to the team's bot on Slack. Once it is connected, a direct message to the bot, or a mention of it in a channel, opens a chat here, and the chat answers in the same thread. Only what you write reaches this hive; what someone else writes goes to theirs.

        Send this code to the bot in a direct message on Slack. It is good for ten minutes, and this box closes on its own once the bot takes it.

        Themes

        A theme paints the whole app and every terminal in it. Click one to wear it — the choice lives in ~/.hive/config.jsonc, next to your fonts and shortcuts. share copies a theme as json to send another hive dev; paste a theme turns a json you received into one of yours — it arrives with its name and colours, and you can change both before keeping it.

        New theme

        Every colour you touch shows up on the screen behind this box, live. save keeps it; cancel puts the old colours back.
